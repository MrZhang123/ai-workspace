# Company Types and Valuation Branches

Classify the company before valuation. A company can have more than one trait, but choose the dominant valuation logic.

## Stable Profitable Company

Traits:

- Mature business.
- Positive earnings and free cash flow.
- Moderate growth.
- Stable margins.

Primary valuation:

- P/E.
- EV/EBIT or EV/EBITDA.
- Free cash flow yield.
- DCF with conservative growth.

Key checks:

- Is growth at least keeping up with inflation and industry growth?
- Are margins stable?
- Is free cash flow close to or higher than net profit?

## High-Quality Compounder

Traits:

- Long-term high ROIC.
- Strong free cash flow.
- Durable competitive advantage.
- Capable management and good capital allocation.

Primary valuation:

- DCF.
- Free cash flow yield.
- P/E or EV/EBIT with quality premium.

Key checks:

- ROIC should be consistently above WACC.
- Growth should not require excessive leverage or dilution.
- Moat should be visible in margins, returns, retention, pricing power, or low customer churn.

## Fast Grower

Traits:

- Revenue and profit grow quickly.
- Market is still expanding.
- Valuation often depends on future scale.

Primary valuation:

- PEG as a rough check.
- DCF with scenario analysis.
- P/S only if profit is temporarily suppressed and unit economics are credible.

Key checks:

- Growth source: more customers, higher price, new products, or acquisitions.
- Growth must not rely mainly on temporary subsidies or one-off cost cuts.
- Actual growth should be compared with sustainable growth.

## Loss-Making Growth Company

Traits:

- Revenue is growing, but net profit and free cash flow are negative.
- Valuation depends on future margin improvement.

Primary valuation:

- Revenue multiple with strict peer and historical comparison.
- Path-to-profit model.
- Cash runway and dilution analysis.
- Scenario DCF only when future margin assumptions are explicit.

Avoid:

- P/E.
- DCF based on far-future positive cash flow without clear assumptions.

Key checks:

- Gross margin trend.
- Operating expense ratio trend.
- Operating cash outflow.
- Cash runway.
- Dilution, convertibles, warrants, or ATM issuance.
- Whether customer demand remains after subsidies fall.

## Cyclical Company

Traits:

- Earnings move with commodity prices, interest rates, capacity cycles, or macro demand.

Primary valuation:

- Normalized earnings across a cycle.
- P/B for asset-heavy firms.
- EV/EBITDA using mid-cycle EBITDA.

Avoid:

- Treating peak earnings as sustainable.
- Treating low P/E at peak cycle as cheap.

Key checks:

- Capacity additions.
- Inventory cycle.
- Product price cycle.
- Balance sheet survival in downturns.

## Turnaround Company

Traits:

- Current results are weak or loss-making.
- Investment case depends on restructuring, asset sales, cost cuts, or business recovery.

Primary valuation:

- Balance sheet and liquidity.
- Breakup value or asset value.
- Normalized earnings after recovery.

Key checks:

- Cash runway.
- Debt maturity.
- One-off versus recurring losses.
- Management credibility.
- Evidence that recovery has started.

## Asset-Based Company

Traits:

- Value mainly comes from cash, investments, property, resource reserves, or portfolio holdings.

Primary valuation:

- Net asset value.
- P/B.
- Sum-of-the-parts.

Key checks:

- Asset liquidity.
- Hidden liabilities.
- Minority interests.
- Tax leakage.
- Management willingness to unlock value.

## Financial Company

Traits:

- Banks, insurers, brokers, asset managers, or lending platforms.

Primary valuation:

- P/B and ROE.
- Net interest margin, credit cost, combined ratio, AUM, or other sector metrics.

Avoid:

- Standard industrial ROIC.
- FCF methods designed for non-financial companies.

Key checks:

- Capital adequacy.
- Asset quality.
- Provisioning.
- Duration and liquidity risk.
- Regulatory limits.
